Why do we have slides in a talk?

“Highest Level Situation”

Verbally: a good place to start is being clear on why we have slides in a talk in the first place…

The goal of any talk is audience impact.

Slides don’t drive impact.

The talk drives impact.

Slides simply reinforce the talk.

Slides are not where a talk starts.

Verbally: Don’t worry! We WILL talk about slides. We just want to be clear that slides are not where a talk starts.

Ask: What can the slides do better than my words?

  • Keep the audience’s attention — the talk is bound by time and attention
  • Understand and care about the information — by directly reinforcing what the speaker is saying
  • Remember it — dual-encoding with multiple senses (hearing and seeing); brain science backs up that using multiple senses aids retention

What slides should not (primarily) do

  • Remind the speaker what they’re supposed to be saying

Caveat: break in case of emergency… they can be a visual aid to keep the speaker on track; that’s just a natural artifact if they do their primary job well!

“Brain breaks” can be useful

Brain breaks (Make it interesting to pay attention):

  • Reveal — show something that is hard to describe (often a moment of revelation)
  • Explain — images can create connections that words cannot
  • Delight — aesthetics keep people interested
