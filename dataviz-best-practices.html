Pixels with Purpose

How to Amplify the Impact of Your Talk Through Visual Reinforcement

Why do we have slides in a talk?

A good place to start is being clear on why we have slides in a talk in the first place…

The goal of any talk is audience impact.

Slides don’t drive impact.

The talk drives impact.

Slides simply reinforce the talk.

Slides are not where a talk starts.

Don’t worry! We WILL talk about slides. We just want to be clear that slides are not where a talk starts.

So what does reinforcement actually mean?

Image source: https://unsplash.com/photos/man-standing-beside-rock-formation-mj51pZwan9c

Hold their attention.

Help them understand what’s being presented.

Enable them to retain what matters most.

  • Keep the audience’s attention — the talk is bound by time and attention
  • Understand and care about the information — by directly reinforcing what the speaker is saying
  • Remember it — dual-encoding with multiple senses (hearing and seeing); brain science backs up that using multiple senses aids retention

What slides should not (primarily) do: remind the speaker what they’re supposed to be (although they can be a visual aid to keep the speaker on track; that’s just a natural artifact if they do their primary job well!)

Some simple tactics can reduce cognitive load

  • Maximize the data-pixel ratio
  • Make the point impossible to miss
  • Keep the audience’s attention

It even works for text!

The application of the revised preprocessing technique that the team developed during the second quarter resulted in a measurable reduction in the overall run-time of the production model, with observed improvements generally falling in the range of approximately 30 to 40 percent.

While this may be an accurate statement, it’s a LOT to read. We’ve all seen slides like this.

The new preprocessing technique reduced model run-time by 30–40%.

The first thing we can do is simply trim the words! Be more economical.

Predicting the body mass of penguins

penguins_clean <- penguins |>
  drop_na(body_mass_g, flipper_length_mm, bill_length_mm, bill_depth_mm) |>
  mutate(body_mass_kg = body_mass_g / 1000,
         species = as.factor(species),
         island = as.factor(island),
         sex = as.factor(sex))

model_spec <- lm(body_mass_kg ~ flipper_length_mm + bill_length_mm +
                   bill_depth_mm + species + sex,
                 data = penguins_clean)

model_summary <- broom::tidy(model_spec, conf.int = TRUE, conf.level = 0.95)

model_summary |>
  filter(term != "(Intercept)") |>
  arrange(desc(abs(estimate)))

This is the default. It may seem fine on your screen now, but it WILL BE TOO MUCH and it WILL BE TOO SMALL when presented

Be careful, though!

Humor can be subtle (and get missed). That’s okay if it’s just a bonus for those who get it. Remember this slide? How many caught that this was “cognitive load” being cheekily represented?

Also, don’t expect all cultural references to land with the entire audience: Star Trek, pop culture, memes.
